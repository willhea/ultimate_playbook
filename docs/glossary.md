# Glossary: ultimate terms

The words this project uses for ultimate, so the coach, the app and the videos
all mean the same thing. Where teams or sources use a word differently, the
entry says so, and says which meaning **we** use. Terms marked **(app)** are
also words on the Play Animator's screen or in its code.

## Directions and the field

- **Field (app).** USA Ultimate: a 70 × 40 yd central zone with a 20 yd end zone at each end, so 110 × 40 yd in all. The app draws this. (An older USA Ultimate guide says 25 yd end zones; the current rules pages say 20.)
- **Central zone.** The part of the field between the end zones. Older rules called it the "playing field proper".
- **Goal line.** The line between the central zone and an end zone. A catch in the end zone you're attacking scores.
- **Brick mark (app).** A spot on the middle of the field, 20 yd out from each goal line. If the pull lands out of bounds, the receiving team can start from the brick. The app draws both bricks.
- **Sideline.** The long edges of the field.
- **Home sideline / away sideline (app).** The two sidelines. **Home** is the side where your team keeps its bags; some teams pick a landmark instead (a parking lot, a building). **Away** is the other side. In the app, home is the bottom of the screen.
- **Downfield / upfield.** Downfield is toward the end zone the offense is attacking (to the right in the app). Upfield, or "back", is toward the disc's own end, behind the thrower.
- **Middle (of the field).** The strip down the center, away from both sidelines.
- **Trap / trap sideline.** When the disc is near a sideline and the force pushes it toward that sideline, the offense is *trapped*: the open side is squeezed against the line.
- **Lane.** A strip of open space a cutter runs into and a throw goes through. In a vertical stack there are two: the **open-side lane** and the **break-side lane**.

## The force and the mark

The force is the defense's plan for the thrower. The mark makes it happen, and every other defender plays off it.

- **Mark / marker (app).** The defender guarding the thrower. (Some teams call the mark in a zone the **rabbit** or **chase**.) In the app, an automatic defender becomes the mark when their player catches the disc.
- **Force (app).** Where the mark pushes the thrower to throw. The mark stands on one side of the thrower to take that side away, which leaves the other side open. The force is named after what's left open.
- **Open side (app).** The side the thrower is pushed toward, where throws are easy. Downfield defenders take away cuts on this side.
- **Break side (app).** The side the mark takes away. Some teams call it the **closed side**.
- **Break / break throw.** A completed throw to the break side, past the mark. The main ways to do it:
  - **Around break.** Around the outside of the mark, on the break side, usually by stepping out wide with the pivot. For our default (right-handed thrower forced to flick) that's a backhand bent around the outside of the mark. The app's "break-side cut from the front" play uses one.
  - **Inside break.** Through the gap between the mark and the open side, then curving back to the break side, usually with an **inside-out** curve.
  - **Over the top.** A hammer or scoober over the mark.
- **Force forehand / force flick.** The mark makes the thrower throw forehands. For a right-handed thrower that means the mark stands on the thrower's left (backhand) side. **Force backhand** is the mirror image.
- **Force home / force away (app).** The mark pushes the thrower toward the home (or away) sideline, whichever hand the thrower uses. Teams call this so the whole defense agrees on a side without checking every thrower's hand.
  - **Our default (decision #8):** force home, with a right-handed thrower facing downfield. Home is the bottom of the screen, so the thrower is forced to flick, and the mark stands on the top (backhand) side.
- **Left-handed throwers.** Everything about forehand and backhand flips. A force home on a left-hander means they throw backhands. That's one reason teams call home/away rather than forehand/backhand. **[check]**: does your team switch the mark for lefties, or stay home/away?
- **Force middle.** The mark pushes the thrower toward the middle of the field, away from the nearer sideline, usually when the disc is near a sideline.
- **Force sideline.** The mark pushes the thrower toward the nearer sideline, to trap them.
- **Straight up.** No force: the mark stands directly in front of the thrower and takes away the throw straight downfield.
- **Flat mark.** The mark stands square, side-on to the downfield direction, to take away throws into the middle and push the disc wide. The **half-flat mark** is a step toward the open side from there.
- **Marking stance.** Feet a bit wider than the shoulders, knees bent, weight on the balls of the feet, hips low, chest open to the thrower, **arms out wide with open hands**. The mark moves their feet to stay in front of the throw, rather than just reaching.
  - The app will draw this: only the mark shows arms, straight out to each side (180° apart), each as long as the player's circle is wide. See [plan.md](plan.md).
- **Stall count / stalling.** The mark counts out loud ("Stalling one, two, …"). If the thrower still has the disc when the mark says "ten", that's a **stall** (a turnover). Outdoor USA Ultimate games count to 10; some small-sided formats count less. The app doesn't model it yet.
- **Disc space.** The mark must stay at least one disc diameter from the thrower's body. Closer is a marking violation.
- **Marking violations.** Breaking any of these marking rules lets the thrower call it, and play carries on while the mark fixes it:
  - **Fast count:** counting faster than one per second, or skipping numbers.
  - **Double team:** a second defender within 10 ft of the thrower without guarding someone there.
  - **Disc space:** standing too close (see above).
  - **Straddle:** the mark's feet on either side of the thrower's pivot foot.
  - **Wrapping:** the mark's arms or legs reaching around the thrower so they're blocked in.
  - **Vision blocking:** the mark using their body to block the thrower's view. The newest rules list this one.
- **Hand block / foot block.** The mark blocks a throw with a hand or a foot.
- **Point block.** The mark knocks a throw down right as it's released.

## Throws

The descriptions are for a right-handed thrower.

- **Backhand.** Thrown across the body, released with the back of the hand toward the target. It comes out on the thrower's left.
- **Forehand / flick (app).** Thrown from the thrower's right side, palm up, like a sidearm throw. "Flick" and "forehand" mean the same thing.
- **Hammer.** An overhead throw with a forehand grip. It flips and flies upside down, over the mark.
- **Scoober.** A short overhead throw with a backhand grip, released across the body. Used to break the mark or get over a zone.
- **Blade.** Thrown tilted steeply, so it drops sharply.
- **Inside-out (IO).** A throw that starts curving toward the thrower's body side, then curves back out. It's tilted so it bends around the mark from the inside.
- **Outside-in (OI).** Starts away from the body and curves back in, like a tilted "around" throw.
- **Huck.** A long throw downfield, usually to a deep cut.
- **Dump.** A short throw backward or sideways to a handler, usually to reset a high stall count.
- **Swing.** A sideways throw that moves the disc across the field, often to the break side. It gains little ground but changes the angle.
- **Upline throw.** A throw down the line, to an upline cut (see cuts).
- **Lead pass.** A throw into space ahead of a running receiver.
- **Hospital pass.** A floaty, hanging throw that invites a collision. **[check]**: whether you use the term.
- **Release point (app).** Where and when the disc leaves the hand. The app sets *when* for each throw ("released 60% into the step").

## Offense: roles and formations

- **Handler.** A player whose main job is to throw and keep the disc moving: the thrower, the dump, the swing. Usually 2–3 per line.
- **Cutter.** A player whose main job is to get open downfield. In a horizontal stack, the middle cutters are the **poppers** and the outside ones the **wings**. Some teams also say **mids** and **deeps**.
- **Dump (player).** The handler behind or beside the thrower, ready for a reset.
- **Stack (app).** How the offense lines up so the lanes stay clear.
  - **Vertical stack ("vert") (app).** The cutters in a line down the middle of the field, with the handlers behind. Cuts come from the **back of the stack**; the exceptions are end-zone sets and break-side cuts, which come from the **front** (decision #7). See [research/stacks.md](research/stacks.md).
  - **Horizontal stack ("ho stack", "3-4") (app).** Three handlers across the field, and four cutters in a line across the field, about 15 yd downfield.
  - **Side stack.** A vertical stack moved to one sideline, which leaves a big open space for one cutter. Often used in the end zone.
  - **Split stack.** Two small stacks, one near each sideline.
  - **Hybrid.** One pair of cutters in a small vertical stack on one side, the rest spread.
- **Iso (isolation).** Clearing space so one chosen cutter goes one-on-one. Often a set play in the end zone.
- **Set play / play.** A called sequence, like the plays in the app.
- **O-line / D-line.** The seven who start a point on offense (receiving the pull) or on defense (pulling). **[check]**: the sources here didn't cover these terms, so this definition is from general knowledge.

## Offense: cuts and movement

- **Cut (app: a run).** A sudden run to get open for a throw.
- **Under cut / in cut (app).** A cut back toward the thrower. Coming under on the open side is the most common completed pass in the game.
- **Deep cut / going deep.** A cut away from the thrower, toward the end zone, for a huck.
- **Out cut.** A cut toward a sideline.
- **Break-side cut.** A cut into the break-side lane. The thrower has to break the mark to hit it.
- **Upline cut ("strike").** A handler's cut from beside or behind the thrower on the break side, running down the open side.
- **Dish / dishy cut.** A dump cut behind the thrower that takes a short reset while already moving toward the break side.
- **Clear (app).** Running out of a lane after a cut that didn't get the disc, so the next cutter has room.
- **Clog.** Standing in someone's lane; the opposite of clearing.
- **Fill.** Moving into a spot someone just left, to keep the shape of the offense. **[check]**: the sources here didn't define it.
- **Continuation.** The next throw after a catch, made while the offense is still moving. Also the cut that sets it up.
- **Give-and-go.** Throw, then cut straight away for the return pass.
- **Reset.** A short pass between handlers that starts a new stall count or changes the angle. "Reset" and "dump" are often used for the same throw.
- **Flow.** The offense moving the disc quickly, catch to catch, without stopping to set up. A style rather than a precise term.
- **Pivot / pivot foot.** The thrower keeps one foot planted and steps with the other to throw around the mark.
- **Travel.** Moving the pivot foot, or taking extra steps after catching. A violation.

## Defense downfield

- **Person defense ("man") (app).** Each defender guards one offensive player. The app's default: defender *n* guards offense *n*.
- **Guarding (app).** Who a defender is assigned to.
- **Under side / deep side.** Standing between your cutter and the disc (**under**, to stop the under cut) or behind them, toward the end zone (**deep**, to stop the huck).
- **Shading.** Where a defender stands relative to their cutter: a little under, a little deep, a little to the open side. The app's default for downfield defenders is a little under and to the open side.
- **Fronting (app: "covered" cut).** Getting fully between the cutter and the disc to deny the under cut. The "clear, then the next cut" play shows D7 fronting O7.
- **Trailing (app).** Chasing a cutter from behind after they've got a step on you. The app's automatic defenders trail a runner a step behind.
- **Reading the hips.** Watching a cutter's hips to tell which way they'll really go; the hips can't fake the way the head and shoulders can. Defenders also keep their own hips open so they can turn and run without crossing their feet. This is a topic for the 3D videos.
- **Bail step.** A defender's first step sideways, running with the cutter without committing their hips one way.
- **Poach.** Leaving your player to guard a lane or space instead, for a moment or as a plan. A calculated risk.
- **Switch.** Two defenders trading the players they guard. **[check]**: the sources here didn't cover this word; some teams say "switch" and others call it out by name.
- **Help.** A defender leaving their player to stop someone else's open cut.

## Zone defense and zone offense

- **Zone.** Defenders guard areas of the field, not players.
- **Cup.** Three defenders close around the thrower: the mark plus two others, so throws through the middle are hard. The cup stops forward throws and makes the offense swing the disc sideways.
- **Wall.** A flat line of defenders across the field instead of a cup.
- **Wings (zone defense).** One near each sideline, behind the cup, stopping throws up the line.
- **Middle-middle.** Just behind the cup, covering the short middle.
- **Short deep / deep-deep.** The last lines of a zone: the short deep stops throws over the cup, and the deep-deep is the last player back.
- **Junk.** A mix of zone and person defense.
- **Fire.** A call to switch from zone to person defense.
- **Poppers (zone offense).** Downfield players working in the gaps around and behind the cup.

## Rules, calls and the flow of a point

- **Pull.** The long throw that starts each point, like a kickoff.
- **Point.** A team scores by catching the disc in the end zone it's attacking. After each point the teams swap directions.
- **Turnover ("turn").** Losing possession, through a dropped or blocked throw, a throw out of bounds, or a stall. The defense is on offense right away.
- **Callahan.** A defender intercepts a throw in the end zone they're attacking. It's a point straight away.
- **Spirit of the game.** Players make their own calls, with fair play expected. Some USA Ultimate games use **observers** to help.
- **Foul.** Contact that matters. Fouls are called by the players.
- **Pick.** A defender blocked by another player while guarding their own, so play stops while they catch up. The app could warn about routes that cause picks. **[check]**: worth adding?

## Big plays

- **Layout.** A full-length dive to catch or block.
- **Bid.** An attempt to catch or block, often diving. Sources differ on whether a bid has to fail.
- **Sky.** Catching the disc at the top of a jump, over someone else.
- **D / block.** A defensive stop: getting a hand, foot or body on the disc to stop a completion. "Getting a D" means making one.
- **Box out.** Using your body position (legally) to keep an opponent away from where the disc will land.

## Play Animator words

- **Play.** A saved sequence: a setup and steps.
- **Setup.** Where everyone starts, before the first step.
- **Step.** One beat of the play: everyone moves from where they were to where they end up. (Football: a "phase".)
- **Run / route.** A player's path in one step. It can be **bent** by dragging its middle dot.
- **Throw.** The disc passing from one teammate to another during a step. It can be bent, and has a **release** time.
- **Facing.** Which way a player looks. **Automatic** turns them sensibly: defenders face their player, offense faces the disc, runners face where they're running. **By hand** fixes it.
- **Automatic / by hand defender.** An automatic defender trails their runner, marks a catcher, or keeps their spacing. Dragging one places them by hand for that step.
- **Close-up / whole field.** Close-up fits the screen to the players; whole field shows all 110 × 40 yd.
- **Present.** A clean full-screen view for recording a video.

## Where sources disagree

- **"Force side".** Some sources mean the side the thrower is pushed toward (the open side); others mean the side the defense takes away. **We don't use "force side".** We say **open side** and **break side**.
- **Field size.** Current USA Ultimate pages say 70 × 40 yd with 20 yd end zones; an older USA Ultimate guide says 25 yd end zones. We use 20.
- **"Home".** Usually your team's bags, but some teams name a landmark. In the app, home is simply the bottom sideline.
- **Dump vs reset.** Often the same throw. Some teams use "dump" for the throw and "reset" for the idea of restarting the count.
- **Zone positions.** Teams name the spots differently: wings or "hammers", cup or "umbrella". Use your team's names in videos.
